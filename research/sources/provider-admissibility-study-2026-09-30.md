# Provider discovery and admissibility study — 2026-09-30

**Scope.** Bounded review of the six OKX-derived Fin Data evidence families that are blocked by the current source-rights contract: perpetual funding, open interest, long/short positioning, mark/index/reference prices for basis, order books, and public trades. The controlling intended use is institutional fund research with durable internal materialization, deterministic derived analytics, and internal AI-assisted analysis. This is an evidence and repository-policy assessment, not legal advice or a provider qualification.

**Disposition.** No reviewed provider/dataset passes both rights and technical suitability. Keep every target family unavailable for admitted research inputs. Do not call providers while rights are BLOCK or UNKNOWN. Existing Fin Data captures are diagnostic, pre-existing observations; this study made no provider calls.

### Candidate inventory result

Within the enabled Fin Data upstream inventory recorded in the project, **OKX is the only provider capable of the exact six derivatives/microstructure families**. No other enabled provider supplies funding, OI, venue positioning, mark/index/perpetual legs, order books, or public trades. Yahoo Finance is the sole observed partial price candidate and is limited to spot-like price history; it is not an equivalent basis source. Alternative.me fear/greed, Cboe, Tiingo, FRED, Finnhub, Alpha Vantage, and other listed macro/equity/prediction/filings providers do not supply the target evidence families.

Massive is the external MCP route already supported by project research, but its dated Basic snapshot and existing capability contract do not provide any of these derivatives or microstructure families. Daily EOD bars can only support a separately declared reference-price input, not mark/index/perpetual basis. Its standard data terms do not authorize institutional use; its business terms are only a path to negotiate the exact product and upstream rights. No additional API/MCP vendor was introduced as a candidate because no such provider is supported by existing project research; expanding the vendor search requires a separately bounded study.
